# Interpretation of results

Figures below are from `cybersecurity_awareness_impact.xlsx` (Impact_Dashboard), 12-question quiz.

## Headline numbers

| Metric | Social media (n=47) | Seminar (n=43) |
|---|---|---|
| Average pre score | 42.9% | 42.4% |
| Average post score | 55.7% | 91.3% |
| Absolute improvement | +12.8 percentage points | +48.8 percentage points |
| Relative improvement (of baseline) | +29.8% | +115.1% |
| Participants whose score increased | 55.3% | 95.3% |
| Substantial / modest / unchanged / lower | 19 / 7 / 12 / 9 | 37 / 4 / 1 / 1 |

Percentage points and relative improvement are different measures: the seminar's +48.8 pp is a 115.1% relative improvement (scores more than doubled) because baselines were low (about 42%).

## Which topics improved most

- **Social media:** Scam Prevention (+17.0 pp) and Phishing (+14.2 pp) gained most. WhatsApp scams gained +9.2 pp.
- **Seminar:** gains were large and fairly even: Everyday Online Fraud +53.5 pp, WhatsApp scams +51.9 pp, Phishing +46.5 pp, Scam Prevention +43.4 pp.
- At question level, the largest gains were QR-code phishing for the social campaign (+19.1 pp) and spotting genuine URLs and screen-sharing scams for the seminar (both +60.5 pp).

## Which intervention showed larger gains

The seminar's gain was nearly four times the social campaign's (+48.8 vs +12.8 pp), more participants improved (95% vs 55%), and fewer scored lower (1 vs 9). The social campaign also left 12 participants unchanged and 9 lower, which reflects noise in a 12-question quiz as well as limited exposure to the posts.

## Where further education is needed

Questions below 60% post accuracy are flagged in the workbook.

- **Social media:** eight of twelve questions remain below 60%. Everyday Online Fraud is weakest (44.7% post). Lowest: UPI collect-request fraud (42.6%), digital arrest (44.7%), and screen-sharing scams and reporting/responding (46.8% each).
- **Seminar:** no question is below 60% post accuracy. The weakest are reporting and responding safely (81.4%) and QR-code phishing (83.7%), so these are the best candidates for extra practice. Digital arrest scams reached 93.0%.
- **Digital arrest scams** stay a weak spot only in the social media scenario (44.7% post, +8.5 pp).

## Caveats

- Quiz length (12 items) means one participant's score moves in 8-point steps, so individual changes are noisy.
- Results depend on the documented model assumptions in the README sheet; changing them changes the findings.
- Pre and post use parallel question versions assumed to be equally difficult; this equivalence is assumed, not tested.
